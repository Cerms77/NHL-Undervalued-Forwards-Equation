Executive summary

Who could add more offense with proper opportunity? This project uses MoneyPuck regular-season data from 2023–24, 2024–25 and 2025–26 to screen potentially undervalued forwards for an increase of at least 0.20 points per game (PPG) the following season—about 16 additional points over 82 games.

Eligibility: NHL forwards with at least 30 games and 250 minutes at five-on-five. Historical comparisons require eligibility in both seasons. Across 727 comparisons, 70 players recorded 71 breakout seasons; Martin Necas did so twice. Both breakout and non-breakout cases informed the model.

How the score works

Score = 1001 + e−S

e ≈ 2.71828 is a fixed mathematical constant—not a value learned from hockey data. It helps convert the weighted result into a score between 0 and 100.

S combines a learned starting value with the six standardized statistics below, each multiplied by its learned weight. −S simply means the negative of that result. The weights remain private.

The six previous-season inputs

  • 5v5 expected goals minus actual goals per 60 minutes.
  • 5v5 primary points per 60 minutes: goals plus first assists.
  • 5-on-4 power-play minutes per game.
  • Overall points per game.
  • 5v5 high-danger shots per 60 minutes.
  • 5v5 on-ice expected-goal percentage: the team’s share of expected goals with the player on the ice.

These inputs were selected through historical analysis. Their inclusion does not mean all six have strong individual correlations or cause a breakout.

What “proper opportunity” means

Working scenarios to assess through scouting and roster context:

  • A regular top-nine role.
  • Approximately 16 minutes of average TOI per game; for a player already at that level, an expanded role adding roughly 1:30–3:00.
  • About 25 additional seconds of average power-play time per game.
  • A team system that fits the player’s strengths and chemistry with linemates.
  • Sustained health and underlying performance as responsibilities grow.

These are guidelines, not proven requirements. The equation does not directly calculate the effects of future opportunity.

How to read the results

Higher scores mean higher estimates within this model. A 90th-percentile ranking means the player scores in approximately the top 10% of eligible forwards—not a 90% chance of breaking out. The top quarter begins at the 75th percentile.

For 2026–27, use 2025–26 inputs. The historical comparisons use 2023–24 inputs for 2024–25 outcomes and 2024–25 inputs for 2025–26 outcomes. No following-season statistics are used as inputs.

The 2025–26 field’s average score is 10.07. The previously discussed 8.09 is the approximate score for a hypothetical player average in all six training inputs, not the field average.
